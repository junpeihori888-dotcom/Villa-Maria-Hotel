Blog 1: The Italian Stop We Didn't Know We Needed

You know that feeling halfway through a big family trip? The excitement of landing in Italy has worn off a little. You've seen the famous squares and the famous churches, eaten more gelato than anyone will admit to, and somewhere between the third museum and the second missed train, someone in the back seat asks, "Are we there yet?" Usually, it's one of the kids. Sometimes it's you.

That's exactly the moment a stop like Villa Maria was made for.

Most families who travel to Italy never hear about Abruzzo. They head for Rome, Florence and the Amalfi Coast, and they fight the crowds to get there. But cross over to the east side of the country, to the Adriatic coast, and something shifts. The beaches get wider. The prices get friendlier. The mountains rise just behind the sea, and the food is the kind Italians themselves drive hours for. It feels less like a postcard and more like real life, the way Italians actually spend their summers.

Francavilla al Mare is one of those real-life beach towns, just south of Pescara. And on a green hill above it, wrapped in a 6-hectare park of old pine and olive trees, sits Villa Maria Hotel & Spa.

The first thing you notice is the quiet.

You pull in, park the car (it's free, and there's plenty of space), and the first thing you notice is how quiet it is. Not empty, just calm. There are shaded tables and sofas scattered through the park, the sea glitters in the distance, and for the first time in days, nobody is rushing anywhere.

The kids, of course, will notice the pool first. It sits in the garden, and within twenty minutes of arriving, they'll likely be in it. That's fine. That gives you twenty minutes too.

A place where everyone gets their thing

The hardest part of any family holiday is that everyone wants something different. The kids want action. You want rest. Your partner wants both, depending on the hour. Villa Maria is one of those rare hotels in Francavilla al Mare where all of that can happen in one place.

In summer, a free shuttle runs down to the hotel's private beach, about 1.5 km away, so beach days don't start with a parking battle. Back at the hotel, there's padel, a fast, fun game somewhere between tennis and squash that kids pick up in about ten minutes and then refuse to stop playing.

And then there's the part for you: the Linfa Spa.

The spa is your reward.

Let's be honest. In most families, one person does the planning. They compare hotels at midnight, read every review, and check the cancellation policy twice. If that's you, the spa is your thank-you.

Every hotel guest gets a complimentary two-hour spa experience; you just book your slot. Inside, there's a hydromassage pool, a Turkish bath, a sauna, a sensory shower, and a quiet room where you can sip herbal tea and do absolutely nothing. If you want more, there are five treatment rooms, including ones for couples, so you and your partner can finally have that massage you keep promising each other.

The spa is mainly for guests aged 12 and up, which keeps it peaceful. So the rhythm that works for most families is simple: one parent takes the kids to the pool or the padel court, the other disappears into the spa, and then you switch. Everyone wins.

Dinner without the debate

After a long day, the last thing anyone wants is a family vote on where to eat. At Villa Maria, you don't have to leave. The restaurant serves Abruzzese and international dishes, meat and fresh Adriatic fish, and there's a kids' menu for the picky eaters. You get to try something local with a glass of wine from the hotel cellar; they get pasta with tomato sauce. Peace at the table.

If you choose Half Board, dinner is already sorted, which on a multi-stop trip feels like a small luxury.

Rooms that actually fit a family

There are Family Suites for families of four, and the team can add cots and bed rails for little ones. If the adults deserve a treat, look at the SPA Suites: the Mini SPA Suite has its own jetted tub, and the Master SPA Suite comes with a private sauna and a sea view. Superior rooms even connect directly to the spa on their floor, so you can slip away in a bathrobe.

Why it works

Villa Maria isn't trying to be the busiest place on your trip. It's the stop where everyone gets to breathe. The kids get their pool, their beach and their padel. You get your spa, your dinner, and your quiet evening on the terrace. And everyone gets what you came to Italy for in the first place: time together, without a schedule chasing you.

If you're looking for the best hotels in Abruzzo, Italy, for a family, this is the one we'd book for the middle of your trip, right when everyone needs it most.

Blog 2: Three Days, Four People, Zero Alarm Clocks

There's a moment on every good holiday when you realise you haven't looked at your phone in hours. You didn't plan it. It just happened. You were too busy watching your kids argue over a padel score, or floating in warm water, or eating something so good that nobody spoke for a minute.

Nobody needs to be told what to do next. The kids are already asking where the pool is.

That first evening is simple. A swim, a shower, and dinner at the hotel restaurant. You try something Abruzzese, maybe grilled meat or fish from the Adriatic, with a glass of Montepulciano. The kids order from their own menu. Everyone's in bed early, and for once, nobody complains about it.

The middle day

You wake up without an alarm. Breakfast runs from 7 to 10, so there's no rush; the buffet has fresh pastries, fruit, and enough choice to keep even the fussiest eater happy.

Then it's beach time. In summer, a free shuttle takes you down to the hotel's private beach, about 1.5 km away. The Adriatic here is calm and gentle near the shore, the kind of sea where younger kids can splash safely while older ones swim a little farther out. You find a spot under an umbrella, open a book you've been carrying for months, and actually read a chapter.

For lunch, you wander along Francavilla's seafront. There's pizza, fresh seafood, and the gelato stop that will become a family tradition by tomorrow.

The afternoon is when the magic happens. Back at the hotel, the kids head to the padel court, a game that's a little like tennis and a little like squash. They've never played before. Within ten minutes, they're competitive. Meanwhile, you slip off to the Linfa Spa for your two-hour slot. There's the hydromassage pool, the Turkish bath, the sauna, and a quiet room with herbal tea. You lie back and realise your shoulders have been up near your ears since March.

Then you swap. Your partner takes the spa; you take the scoreboard.

That evening, if the kids are old enough to manage on their own for an hour, it's the perfect time for the couples' massage you booked. The hotel has treatment rooms designed for two. Afterwards, dinner as a family, with sunset turning the sea pink.

The slow last day

On the third morning, you take a short drive. Five minutes away is the Pineta Dannunziana, a pine forest reserve with easy, shaded walking paths. Or you head into Pescara to walk the long seafront and cross the Ponte del Mare, a bridge built just for walkers and cyclists.

By early afternoon, you're back. Maybe it's a padel rematch, a last swim, or a nap. Day three belongs to whatever your family loved most.

At the final dinner, you ask the kids what their favourite part was. The answers will surprise you. Write them down. They're the reason you'll come back.

The goodbye

Check-out is by 11 AM, but there's no need to rush to the car. The front desk can hold your bags while you take one last walk through the park or one last swim. Express checkout means you won't have to wait in line either.

And as you drive off toward your next stop, you'll notice something. Everyone's quieter, but in a good way. Rested. Closer. That's what three days at Villa Maria can do.
